Getting the integration right technically is half the job. How you introduce and talk about rewards determines whether players engage, claim, build a daily habit, and tell their friends. This page is for game designers and marketing — the people writing copy, designing flows, and sending notifications.

## Start with the right players

<Warning>
**The single biggest lever on conversion isn't copy — it's acquisition.** Your best results come from running UA aimed at players who *want to earn*. Target them deliberately: rewarded-play audiences, earning-focused creatives and channels, messaging that leads with real rewards.

A player who installed your game with no interest in earning is hard to convert no matter how good the rewards UX is. Nothing on this page will fix a mismatched audience — but everything on it compounds when the audience is right.
</Warning>

## Recommendations

Four things move the needle most, in this order:

1. **Give the first reward early.** Within the first minutes of normal play, before the player has gone looking for a rewards feature. Discovering a balance that already exists is what makes it feel real — and it means they never see the counter sitting at zero.
2. **Explain how rewards work.** Players need to know what they've earned and why it's real. Do it in your own game UI, or with a [creative](/earn/sdk/creatives) — a dashboard-configured modal in your art style that you can change without an app release.
3. **Remind players who have unclaimed earnings.** A balance sitting unclaimed is your easiest win-back. Reach them with [local notifications](/earn/sdk/local-notifications) or your own push stack while they're away, and with a [creative](/earn/sdk/creatives) when they return.
4. **Show progress toward the next reward.** A near, named goal ("80% of the way to a \$5 gift card") converts where an abstract balance doesn't. [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) gives you the progress value.

The rest of this page is the detail behind these: the principle they all follow, then what to say at each moment.

## The one principle: reveal, don't offer

Players installed your game to play, not to earn. A rewards pitch they haven't earned anything from yet is a cold offer — and cold offers trigger suspicion, not desire. An unexplained reward reads as a hidden cost: *what's the catch?*

So don't *offer* rewards. **Reveal them.** The player should discover they've already earned something by doing the thing they came to do. Every screen and sentence should reinforce: this is part of the game you already like, it's already working, and here's why it's real.

Two rules follow from this:

1. **Never show a zero balance as the player's first exposure.** Fire the first reward within the first minutes of normal play, so by the time the player notices the counter, something is already in it. "You've already earned 240 coins" converts; "Start earning!" doesn't.
2. **Always pair the reward with the honest reason.** An unexplained reward feels like a scam. One sentence kills the suspicion:

<Note>
**The reason line — the single highest-leverage sentence in your rewards copy:**

*"Game studios share their ad revenue with players. The more you play, the more you earn."*

The modal's built-in first-time screen doesn't carry this line, so ship it yourself: a [first-open creative](/earn/sdk/creatives) is the ideal vehicle — it shows in your game's art style and you can update it without an app release. Never present the reward without the reason.
</Note>

## Core principles

**Speak in your game's voice, and keep ZBD invisible until it adds credibility.** The player trusts the game they chose to install; the rewards layer should inherit that trust, not spend it. Your fonts, your art, your currency, your tone. ZBD appears at the payout step, where a real payment processor makes the money credible — never in headers or gameplay copy.

**Pair your currency with its real-money value.** Lead with your themed currency — it keeps rewards native to your game and lets you be generous with the numbers — and show the dollar value alongside it: players are ultimately motivated by real money. "You earned 2,500 coins (\$0.25)" gives them both. Concrete dollar milestones remain your strongest motivator: *"Next reward: \$5 gift card — 80% there."*

**Show progress to the *next* milestone, never a far summit.** A close goal accelerates play; a distant one demotivates. [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) gives you `unlockedPercent` for exactly this — bind it to a progress bar toward a named, concrete reward.

**Say "claim", not banking verbs, at first touch.** "Claim your reward" feels like winning; "withdraw" and "cash out" feel like banking, and banking feels like risk. Reserve cash-out vocabulary for the payout flow itself. (The modal's built-in CTA currently reads "Cash Out" — that copy is fixed product UI inside the claim flow; the vocabulary *you* control on the way there should say claim.)

**Send players to the modal when there's something worth claiming.** The modal opens on their balance and cashout options, so it lands best once there's a balance worth acting on — a player sent there with nothing to claim just sees an empty wallet. Never gate the value reveal behind sign-up: players don't need any account to see what they've earned.

## The journey, in five moments

| Moment | What happens | Your tool |
|--------|--------------|-----------|
| **Earn** | "+40 coins" as ambient feedback while playing — noticed, not interrupting. Tie triggers to high-emotion gameplay moments. | Your game UI + `SendReward` |
| **Reveal** | One screen, after the first reward has landed: the balance they already have, the reason line, one CTA ("Keep playing"). | A [creative](/earn/sdk/creatives) |
| **Progress** | Balance always visible; progress bar toward the next named reward. | Your game UI + [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) |
| **Claim** | Player taps through with a balance worth claiming, picks a payout option, and celebrates like a level-up, not a receipt. | The ZBD modal (`ShowModal`) |
| **Return** | Milestone reminders while away; tomorrow's fresh earning allowance framed as an opportunity. | [Local notifications](/earn/sdk/local-notifications) + creatives |

## Communication touchpoints

| Touchpoint | When | Goal | Sample copy |
|-----------|------|------|------------|
| First reward | First reward event, session 1 | Make it feel real, ambiently | "+500 [Currency]" toast — no modal, no interruption |
| Reveal | Right after the first reward | Pair value with the reason | "You've already earned 500 [Currency] (\$0.05). This game shares its ad revenue with players — the more you play, the more you earn." |
| Progress milestone | Meaningful % toward a reward | Keep the next goal near | "You're 80% of the way to a $5 gift card." |
| First claim | Balance worth claiming | Convert | "Your first reward is ready to claim." |
| Limit reached | Player has withdrawn all their limit currently allows | Turn a ceiling into retention | "You've maxed out today's rewards. Come back tomorrow to earn more." |
| Re-engagement | Lapsed player (3+ days) | Re-activation | "Your [X] [Currency] are still waiting — come and claim them." |

## Withdrawal limit messaging

The daily earning allowance is your most powerful retention mechanic — use it that way.

When a player has withdrawn everything their limit currently allows, the message is: **"Come back tomorrow to earn more."** Not "You've reached your limit." Not "Withdrawal limit: \$0.20." Their allowance to unlock more resets every 24 hours — frame that as tomorrow's opportunity, not today's restriction.

## Push notifications

Same copy model as [local notifications](/earn/sdk/local-notifications) — concrete goal over bare number, claim over cash out:

| Scenario | Sample copy |
|---------|------------|
| Unclaimed rewards | "You've got [X] [Currency] waiting — come and claim them." |
| Near a milestone | "You're [X]% of the way to a [card] gift card. Keep playing to unlock it." |
| Reward ready | "You've unlocked a [card] gift card — come and claim it." |
| New day, fresh allowance | "You can earn more rewards today. Time to play." |
| Day 7 streak milestone | "7-day streak! A special reward is in your daily chest." |

No urgency theater ("Limited time! Act now!") — urgency reads as manipulation to someone who didn't come to shop.

## In-app messages

| Scenario | Sample copy |
|---------|------------|
| Rewards feature launch (existing players) | After their next reward lands: "You've already earned [X] [Currency] — [Game Name] now shares its ad revenue with players. Keep playing to unlock real rewards." |
| Post-first-reward | "You just earned your first [Currency]! [X] more and your first reward is ready to claim." |
| Approaching a milestone | "Almost there — [X] [Currency] until your $5 gift card." |
| After a long session | "Great session! You earned [X] [Currency] today. Keep playing tomorrow for bonus rewards." |

Avoid full-screen takeovers at launch — a pitch before play is a cold offer. Let the first reward land, then reveal.

## Say this, not that

| Avoid | Say instead | Why |
|-------|------------|-----|
| "Earn real money!" | "Your playtime just paid out" / "You've already earned [X] [Currency]" | Ad-speak triggers skepticism; already-happened is a fact |
| "Withdraw" / "Cash out" (first touch) | "Claim your reward" | Banking verbs feel like risk; claiming feels like winning |
| "Sats", "Bitcoin", "wallet", "Lightning" | "Your rewards" | Crypto vocabulary spooks a casual audience — keep it behind the payout-methods door (Bitcoin-native games excepted) |
| "Sign up to start earning" | "You've already earned [X] [Currency]" | Never gate the value reveal behind effort |
| A far-off summit ("Save up for a \$100 payout") | "Next reward: \$5 gift card — 80% there" | Near milestones motivate; distant goals demotivate |
| "Limited time! Act now!" | "New bonus round on your next game" | Urgency reads as manipulation |
| "ZBD Rewards" as a header | "[Game]'s Reward Vault" (or Loot, Treasury…), "powered by ZBD" small at the payout step | A third-party brand is a context switch |
| "You earned 1,200 [Currency]" (value hidden) | "You earned 1,200 [Currency] (\$0.12)" | Players are motivated by real money — show the value with the currency |
| "Rewards unavailable" (player hasn't earned) | Hide rewards UI silently | Nothing to miss, nothing to explain |
| "ZBD is down" | "Rewards are temporarily unavailable" (players with a balance only) | Never name the platform; never alarm players who haven't started |

Tone throughout: the voice of the *game*, not of a fintech. Short, warm, second person, zero exclamation-mark salesmanship. One idea per screen.
