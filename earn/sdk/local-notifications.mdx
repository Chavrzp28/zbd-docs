A player who leaves with an uncashed balance is the easiest player to win back. Local notifications let you remind them — *"you have earnings waiting"*, *"you're 80% of the way to an Amazon card"* — without any push infrastructure.

Because the OS holds the scheduled notification, there is **no server, no device tokens, and no backend work**. You schedule when the player leaves and cancel when they return, so a reminder only ever fires if they genuinely stayed away.

<Warning>
**Local notifications are not an SDK feature — you build them.** The Earn SDK has no notification API. What it gives you is the *data* worth notifying about: the player's balance and their progress toward a gift card. Scheduling, wording, and timing are all your own code.
</Warning>

## Who provides what

| Piece | Provided by |
|---|---|
| Reward data — balance and gift-card progress | **ZBD Earn SDK** — [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) or [`GetBalance`](/earn/sdk/user-balance) |
| Scheduling, permissions, and display | **Unity's Mobile Notifications package** (`com.unity.mobile.notifications`) |
| The reminder controller, cadence, and copy | **You** |

<Info>
A working reference implementation ships in the ZBD SDK **demo project** as `ZBDDemoNotifications.cs`. It is demo code, not part of the SDK package — copy it into your game as a starting point and adapt it. The rest of this page explains the pattern it implements and the pitfalls it works around, so you can build your own with confidence.
</Info>

<Note>
The demo project uses Mobile Notifications version `2.4.3`. Add the package to your own project before implementing any of this.
</Note>

## The pattern

Three moments matter:

<Steps>
<Step title="During play — cache the player's reward state">
After anything that changes the player's balance — after `Init`, after a reward is credited, after a withdrawal — read their state from the SDK and store it locally (`PlayerPrefs` is enough).
</Step>
<Step title="On background — schedule the reminders">
When the app is backgrounded, build your reminder copy from that cached state and hand the notifications to the OS.
</Step>
<Step title="On return — cancel everything pending">
When the player comes back, cancel all pending reminders. Anything still scheduled is therefore only reaching players who stayed away.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**You cannot fetch at schedule time.** `OnApplicationPause` is not a safe place for network calls — the app is suspending and the request won't reliably finish. That's why you cache state *during play*, and why your reminder reflects the balance as of the player's last session.
</Warning>

## Reading the reward state

Call the SDK during play and cache what you need. [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) is the better choice, because a single call gives you both the balance and how close the player is to a specific card — which makes for a much stronger reminder than a bare number:

```csharp
// Your own class — the SDK has no notification API
ZBDController.Instance.GetGiftCards(response =>
{
    if (response != null && response.success)
    {
        ZBDGiftCard best = null;
        foreach (ZBDGiftCard card in response.giftCards ?? new ZBDGiftCard[0])
        {
            // unlockedPercent is -1 when progress couldn't be computed
            if (card != null && card.unlockedPercent >= 0f &&
                (best == null || card.unlockedPercent > best.unlockedPercent))
            {
                best = card;
            }
        }

        CacheState(response.availableBalanceInSats, best?.name, best?.unlockedPercent ?? -1f);
        return;
    }

    // Gift cards unavailable (rewards UI not loaded yet) —
    // the balance alone still makes a perfectly good reminder
    ZBDController.Instance.GetBalance(balance =>
    {
        if (balance != null && balance.success)
        {
            CacheState(balance.balance, null, -1f);
        }
    });
});
```

Where `CacheState` is your own method writing to `PlayerPrefs`. If your game already tracks the player's balance, you can skip the SDK round trip entirely and cache your own value.

Call this from two places: after a successful `Init` where the player is a signed-in rewards user, and again whenever their balance changes — so a reminder never tells a player who has already cashed out that they have earnings waiting.

## Asking for permission

<Warning>
**Never ask on a cold launch.** iOS shows the system permission prompt only once — if the player declines, the only way back is the Settings app. A badly timed first ask costs you the channel permanently.
</Warning>

Ask at a moment the request makes sense to the player: after their first reward, or once they're a signed-in rewards user. Guard it so you only ever prompt once per install.

On **Android 13 (API 33) and above** the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` runtime permission is required. Without it nothing is shown and nothing errors — notifications silently never appear.

Android also needs a notification channel registered before anything can be posted (API 26+). Registering an existing channel again is a no-op, so it's safe to do on every launch.

## Scheduling and cancelling

Schedule on **pause**, not on quit:

```csharp
private void OnApplicationPause(bool paused)
{
    if (paused)
        ScheduleReminders();   // your method
    else
        CancelAllReminders();  // your method
}
```

<Note>
Use `OnApplicationPause`, **not** `OnApplicationQuit`. Android frequently kills a backgrounded process without ever calling `Quit`, and iOS calls it rarely — scheduling there means reminders that never get armed. Pause is the reliable "the player left" signal.
</Note>

Always cancel before rescheduling. A player may background, return, and background again, which leaves the balance behind the old reminders stale.

## Writing your reminder copy

**The messages are yours to write.** The demo tiers its copy by how close the player is to a card, preferring a concrete goal over a bare number — *"80% of the way to an Amazon card"* pulls far harder than a point balance. It's a reasonable model to start from:

| Player state | Example title | Example body |
|---|---|---|
| 50–99% toward a specific card | Almost there! | You're 80% of the way to a `{card}` gift card. Keep playing to unlock it. |
| 100% or more toward a card | Your gift card is ready | You've unlocked a `{card}` gift card — come and claim it. |
| Has a balance, no card progress | You have earnings to cash out | You've got `{balance}` `{currency}` waiting. Come and cash out. |
| Balance is zero | — | Nothing is scheduled. |

Use whatever your players call your currency — points, coins, sats — not ZBD's terminology.

<Warning>
**If the balance is zero, say nothing.** A player who has already cashed out must never be told they have earnings waiting.
</Warning>

See the [Player Communication Guide](/earn/sdk/player-comms) for wording that stays on the right side of app store and advertising rules.

## Cadence

The demo schedules three reminders at **24, 72, and 168 hours** (1 day, 3 days, 1 week) after the player leaves. Since all pending reminders are cancelled the moment they return, the later entries only ever reach players who stayed away that long.

Pick your own timings. Keep in mind:

- Both platforms batch very short timers, so anything under **60 seconds** is unreliable.
- On Android, prefer **inexact** delivery — it's fine for a retention nudge and avoids the `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` permission that Google Play requires justification for.
- On iOS, set `ShowInForeground = false` — don't interrupt a player who is already in your game with a reminder to come back to it.

## Testing

Notifications are **device-only** — they don't fire in the Unity Editor.

To test the flow without waiting a day, do what the demo does and add a debug flag that fires reminders seconds after backgrounding instead of hours. Never ship it enabled.

## Pairing with creatives

Local notifications bring the player back; a [creative modal](/earn/sdk/creatives) tells them what to do next. Showing a *"you're halfway to a gift card"* creative on the session that a reminder brought them back to is a natural pairing — both can be driven from the same `GetGiftCards` progress value.
