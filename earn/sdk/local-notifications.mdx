A player who leaves with an uncashed balance is the easiest player to win back. Local notifications let you remind them — *"you have earnings waiting"*, *"you're 80% of the way to an Amazon card"* — without any push infrastructure.

Because the OS holds the scheduled notification, there is **no server, no device tokens, and no backend work**. You schedule when the player leaves and cancel when they return, so a reminder only ever fires if they genuinely stayed away.

<Note>
Requires Unity's **Mobile Notifications** package (`com.unity.mobile.notifications`). The SDK demo project uses version `2.4.3`.
</Note>

<Info>
A complete reference implementation ships in the SDK demo project as `ZBDDemoNotifications.cs`. It is **demo code, not part of the SDK** — copy it into your game and adapt the wording, timings, and currency label to fit.
</Info>

## How it works

Three moments matter:

<Steps>
<Step title="During play — cache the player's reward state">
Call `RefreshRewardState()` after anything that changes the player's balance: after `Init`, after a reward is credited, and after a withdrawal. It stores the balance and gift-card progress in `PlayerPrefs`.
</Step>
<Step title="On background — schedule the reminders">
When the app is backgrounded, reminders are built from that cached state and handed to the OS.
</Step>
<Step title="On return — cancel everything pending">
When the player comes back, all pending reminders are cancelled. Anything still scheduled is therefore only reaching players who stayed away.
</Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
**Scheduling cannot fetch.** `OnApplicationPause` is not a safe place for network calls — the app is suspending and the request won't reliably finish. That's why state is cached *during play* and persisted to `PlayerPrefs`, and why the reminder reflects the balance as of the player's last session.
</Warning>

## Wiring it up

Two integration points. After a successful `Init` where the player is a signed-in rewards user, cache their state and ask for permission:

```csharp
ZBDController.Instance.Init(completion =>
{
    if (!completion.success) return;

    ZBDDemoNotifications.Instance.RefreshRewardState();
    ZBDDemoNotifications.Instance.RequestPermission();
});
```

And again whenever the balance changes, so a reminder never tells a player who has already cashed out that they have earnings waiting:

```csharp
// after a reward is credited, or after a withdrawal completes
ZBDDemoNotifications.Instance.RefreshRewardState();
```

`RefreshRewardState` uses [`GetGiftCards`](/earn/sdk/gift-cards) because a single call yields both the balance and how close the player is to a specific card — which makes for a much stronger reminder. It falls back to [`GetBalance`](/earn/sdk/user-balance) if the rewards UI hasn't loaded yet.

If your game already tracks the player's balance, you can skip the SDK round trip and cache it directly:

```csharp
ZBDDemoNotifications.Instance.CacheState(balance, giftCardName, giftCardPercent);
```

## Asking for permission

<Warning>
**Never ask on a cold launch.** iOS shows the system permission prompt only once — if the player declines, the only way back is the Settings app. A badly timed first ask costs you the channel permanently.
</Warning>

Ask at a moment the request makes sense to the player: after their first reward, or once they're a signed-in rewards user. `RequestPermission()` is safe to call repeatedly — it only ever prompts once per install.

On **Android 13 (API 33) and above** the `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` runtime permission is required. Without it nothing is shown and nothing errors — notifications silently never appear.

## Scheduling and cancelling

Schedule on **pause**, not on quit:

```csharp
private void OnApplicationPause(bool paused)
{
    if (paused)
        ScheduleReminders();
    else
        CancelAllReminders();
}
```

<Note>
Use `OnApplicationPause`, **not** `OnApplicationQuit`. Android frequently kills a backgrounded process without ever calling `Quit`, and iOS calls it rarely — scheduling there means reminders that never get armed. Pause is the reliable "the player left" signal.
</Note>

Always cancel before rescheduling. A player may background, return, and background again, which leaves the balance behind the old reminders stale.

## What the reminder says

The demo picks its copy from the cached state, preferring a concrete goal over a bare number — *"80% of the way to an Amazon card"* pulls far harder than a point balance:

| Player state | Title | Body |
|---|---|---|
| 50–99% toward a specific card | Almost there! | You're 80% of the way to a `{card}` gift card. Keep playing to unlock it. |
| 100% or more toward a card | Your gift card is ready | You've unlocked a `{card}` gift card — come and claim it. |
| Has a balance, no card progress | You have earnings to cash out | You've got `{balance}` `{currency}` waiting. Come and cash out. |
| Balance is zero | — | Nothing is scheduled. |

<Warning>
**If the balance is zero, say nothing.** A player who has already cashed out must never be told they have earnings waiting.
</Warning>

## Cadence

The demo schedules three reminders at **24, 72, and 168 hours** (1 day, 3 days, 1 week) after the player leaves. Since all pending reminders are cancelled the moment they return, the later entries only ever reach players who stayed away that long.

Tune these to your game. Keep in mind:

- Both platforms batch very short timers, so anything under **60 seconds** is unreliable.
- On Android the demo uses **inexact** delivery, which is fine for a retention nudge and avoids the `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` permission that Google Play requires justification for.
- On iOS the notification sets `ShowInForeground = false` — don't interrupt a player who is already in your game with a reminder to come back to it.

## Testing

Notifications are **device-only** — they don't fire in the Unity Editor.

To test the flow without waiting a day, the demo exposes `debugFastReminders`, which fires reminders seconds after backgrounding instead of hours.

<Warning>
Never ship with `debugFastReminders` enabled.
</Warning>

## Pairing with creatives

Local notifications bring the player back; a [creative modal](/earn/sdk/creatives) tells them what to do next. Showing a *"you're halfway to a gift card"* creative on the session that a reminder brought them back to is a natural pairing — both can be driven from the same `GetGiftCards` progress value.
